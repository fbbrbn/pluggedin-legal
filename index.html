PluggedIn

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 July 2026

PluggedIn charges your electric car when electricity is cheapest. This page explains exactly what data that involves, where it goes, and what control you have over it.

The short version

PluggedIn has no servers. There is no PluggedIn account, no login, and no database anywhere holding your information. Everything the app knows about you stays on your phone.

We do not collect, receive, store, sell, or share your personal data — we genuinely never see it. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind in this app.

The app does talk to other companies' services — your charger's cloud, your car's cloud, a public charger directory — but only the ones you choose to connect, and it talks to them directly from your phone.

2. What data the app handles, and why

2.1 Location — only in "Find a Charger"

What: your device's approximate and precise location.

When: only while you have the Find a Charger screen open. Never in the background, and never if you don't use that feature.

Why: to show public charging stations near you and rank them by distance and price.

Where it goes: your coordinates are sent to OpenChargeMap to retrieve nearby stations, and map tiles are requested from OpenStreetMap, which necessarily reveals the area you are viewing. Your location is not stored by PluggedIn, not written to a file, and not logged.

Legal basis: your consent, given through the Android location permission. You can withdraw it at any time in Android Settings; the rest of the app keeps working without it.

2.2 Your charger account (Easee)

What: the email address and password you enter to sign in to Easee, which are exchanged immediately for access tokens; then your charger's ID, status, and charging session history.

Why: to start and stop charging at the right time, and to show what your charging actually cost.

Where it goes: directly to Easee's cloud service (Norway, within the EEA). Your Easee password is used only for that sign-in and is not stored. The resulting tokens are kept encrypted on your device.

Legal basis: necessary to provide the service you asked for.

2.3 Your vehicle account (Tesla, Tessie, or Smartcar — optional)

What: authorisation tokens for the service you connect, and your car's battery percentage.

Why: so charging can stop at your target battery level instead of overcharging.

Where it goes: directly to whichever you connect — Tesla, Tessie, or Smartcar. The app requests only battery level and basic vehicle information. It does not request or store your VIN, location, driving history, or any other vehicle data.

Legal basis: your consent. This feature is entirely optional; the app schedules charging perfectly well without it.

2.4 Electricity prices and grid carbon intensity

What: your price area only — DK1 or DK2, which covers roughly half of Denmark each.

Where it goes: Energi Data Service and Energinet. No personal data is involved; these requests say nothing about you beyond which half of the country you are in.

2.5 Your settings and charging history

What: your car model, charger, electricity tariff, schedules, and past charging sessions.

Where it goes: nowhere. This is stored on your device only.

If you have Android Auto Backup enabled, Android may copy app settings to your own Google Drive, under your Google account. That is a feature of Android, controlled by you in your device settings, and the backup is not accessible to PluggedIn. Your charger and vehicle tokens are deliberately excluded from it.

2.6 Notifications

Generated on your device. No notification service or push provider is involved, and nothing about your charging leaves the phone to produce them.

2.7 The "what you could have saved" figure

When you first connect your charger, the app imports your past charging sessions and calculates what optimised charging would have saved you. This calculation happens entirely on your device. Nothing is transmitted, and no aggregate is shared with anyone.

If a future version offers to share anonymised statistics, it will be strictly opt-in, off by default, and described here before it ships.

3. What the app does not do

  • No advertising, ad networks, or ad identifiers
  • No analytics or usage tracking
  • No crash or diagnostics reporting
  • No PluggedIn account, and no PluggedIn server
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone
  • No profiling or automated decisions with legal effects
  • Nothing is collected from children, and the app is not directed at them

4. Data leaving the EU/EEA

Some services you may connect operate outside the EEA — notably Tesla, Tessie, and Smartcar (United States), and OpenChargeMap (United Kingdom).

Those transfers happen because you chose to connect that account, and they are governed by that company's own privacy policy and terms, which apply directly between you and them. PluggedIn does not route your data through any intermediary. Easee operates within the EEA.

We recommend reading the privacy policy of any service before connecting it.

5. How long data is kept

On your device, until you delete it. Because there is no server, there is nothing for us to retain, and nothing for us to delete on your behalf.

To erase everything:

  • Delete individual charging sessions in the app's history, or
  • Disconnect your charger or vehicle in Setup, which deletes the stored tokens, or
  • Uninstall PluggedIn, which removes all app data from your device

Data already held by Easee, Tesla, Tessie, or Smartcar belongs to your account with them and must be deleted through them.

6. Security

Charger and vehicle access tokens are stored using Android's Keystore-backed encrypted storage, not in plain text. Those keys are bound to your device, which is also why the tokens do not survive a transfer to a new phone — you simply sign in again.

All communication with external services uses HTTPS.

No system is perfectly secure, but the strongest protection here is structural: there is no central database to breach, because there is no central database.
